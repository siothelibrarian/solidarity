## What is this resolution?
The Negotiator Workday Limits resolution aims to start a conversation within MAPE about how we ensure that our negotiators are not subject to inhumane and anti-worker practices such as "Push Week", where MMB routinely expects our members and staff to be on-call until they are ready to present proposals or engage in discussions. 

In the last round of negotiations, MMB engaged in this stalling tactic multiple times - from their initial "offer" delivered at 6:45 PM on the first day of Push Week to the final 20-hour bargaining session that resulted in our current contract. This is clearly a tactic that is designed to wear us down and make us agree to concessions that we would not otherwise agree to - and we don't have to go along with it. When our negotiators are bargaining with MMB, they are working - and they deserve the same rights and dignity that we would expect when we are performing our state jobs.

Right now, the resolution proposes placing limits on the hours that our negotiators do their work, so that they are not forced to work more than 8 hours within a 24-hour period, and limiting to work to weekdays between 6 AM and 10 PM. **It is explicitly and enthusiastically open to discussion and radical change**. 

I think that we can all recognize that change is needed if we want to be successful in getting our membership engaged and energized for the next contract cycle. We cannot have change without discussion, without examination of the assumptions we take for granted, or without considering new ideas, even if they differ from the strategies that were used in the past.

This resolution is **not** an attack on our negotiators, a referendum on their work, or an attempt to undermine their expertise. They have a hard job, and they do valuable and valued work to make sure our members are represented and protected. We should offer them the same respect and support.

### Isn't it a bad idea to limit what our negotiators can do?
No. Laws, contracts, and policies serve a dual purpose - they limit what we can do, but they also limit what we can be coerced into doing by employers. 

Unions were formed because workers realized that if they did not set limits on what they would do and bargain collectively to enforce those limits, employers would push individuals and small groups to work longer, harder, and for less money. They would insist on working conditions that were convenient for them, regardless of how those conditions affected workers' health, safety, or ability to spend time with their families.

We have a responsibility to make sure that all of the employees we represent have safe and healthy working conditions, whether they are performing their roles for the state or engaging in union activities.

### I agree that this is a problem. But I don't agree that this is the solution.
Propose something else, then. Resolutions are subject to amendment, and this one is no different. The best solutions to problems are usually collaborative and creative - and I am more than willing to hear from people who have different perspectives and experience from mine. 

My goal is to start a conversation that begins to address this problem - I'm open to any effective solution, even if it's just a step in the right direction. These sorts of complex problems aren't fixed easily or overnight - but that doesn't mean that we should assume they are something we just have to deal with. Nothing will ever improve with that attitude.

### I don't think this is a problem. 
I don't think I've ever heard that before. Even when people argue that Push Week is necessary or important or inevitable, they all agree it sucks. 

## Frequently-Heard Feedback
Here are some common/oft-repeated things I've heard as I've talked about this resolution, and some responses. Do you have more? Let me know!

### We have to participate in Push Week - it's just part of the negotiations process and there's nothing we can do about it.
Nope! The Public Employment Labor Relations Act (PELRA) says that we have to meet and negotiate with the employer in good faith to agree on terms and conditions of employment **at reasonable times**. (emphasis added). See: [MN Statutes 179A.03, Subd. 11.](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/179A.03#stat.179A.03.11).

To be clear - I am not a lawyer. But I cannot find anything in PELRA that requires us to submit to any capricious whim that MMB has just because we're in negotiations. And the very first paragraph of the statute says this:

> This policy is subject to the paramount right of the citizens of this state to keep inviolate the guarantees for their health, education, safety, and welfare.

We remain citizens of Minnesota, and of this country, even at the bargaining table. More importantly, we remain **employees**, which means we should be protected by the laws that govern working conditions at both the state and federal levels.

### We have to bargain with AFSCME, otherwise they will bargain without us.
They might, and that's their right. We're two different unions; we each have to make choices that are best for our members and we're subject to different limitations. But it's not in their best interest to bargain without us, and they know that. We're actively working to build a better relationship with them - maybe this is something we should talk about with them.

Their negotiators don't like the conditions of Push Week, either. I've shared this resolution and had discussions with the AFSCME negotiators and leaders that I'm close with, and they're interested in considering something similar policies.

### Our contract expires on June 30th - we have to make sure we have a tentative agreement before the expiration.
The Public Employment Labor Relations Act (PELRA), which governs us, says that our contract stays in effect even after it is expired. In fact, our contract has to expire for us to have the right to strike - so there's no reason for us to be pressured into an agreement with a false sense of urgency. See: [MN Statute 179A.20, Subd. 6](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/179A.03#stat.179A.03.11)

### It's not appropriate for anyone but the negotiators to dictate how they work.
1. If MMB is setting Push Week, they are dictating how negotiators work. 
2. This resolution is intended for discussion at Delegate Assembly, MAPE's highest governing body. Resolutions at Delegate Assembly can establish policies for any group within MAPE, and no part of MAPE is exempt from its authority. 
3. We already limit how negotiators work. We limit the amount of lost time they can take, we set rules for how they are elected, and we require them to follow our code of conduct and other policies.

Having negotiators participate in addressing the issues they face and crafting solutions will make sure those solutions are effective, and I would love to have them be authors on this resolution. Let's set our own boundaries for our work, instead of having them imposed upon us.

### Participating in Push Week is part of our bargaining strategy.
Is it a good strategy, though? Are we not able to think of different strategies? Do we not think that we **need** different strategies, considering what our current contract looks like? Our last contract cycle left many members and negotiators disillusioned and disengaged, and we will have to work hard to build our strength for next cycle. 

Many unions have chosen to move away from opaque, secretive bargaining in favor of more transparent, engaging processes. Generally, unions have reported that they receive better contracts when they do this (although that's mostly anecdotal). But there are many ways to strategize around bargaining; you can see some examples in this report from the UC Berkeley Labor Center: [Turning the Tables: Participation and Power in Negotiations](https://laborcenter.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Turning-the-Tables.pdf)

What research we do have around negotiations can be summarized as follows:
- Humans have a limited ability to focus on tasks. Overwork, sleep deprivation, and time pressure contribute to reduced critical thinking skills, lack of creativity and impulse control, and an increase in mistakes.
- Vague and contradictory language in collective bargaining agreements is a common driver of grievances and arbitrations.
- Research shows that cognitive processing is altered when decisions are made under time pressure, and this can lead to more concessions and less exploration of alternative solutions to problems. The longer a negotiation goes on, the more chance there is to come to an agreement that is acceptable to both parties. [The Impact of Time Pressure and Information on Negotiation Process and Decisions](https://link.springer.com/10.1023/A:1008736622709)

### Some other things to consider
- Negotiators can only be paid for 8 hours of lost time per day, no matter how long they work.
- Our staff get paid based on their contracts, but they still have to be there to support us. 
- How much does Push Week cost us, and are there better ways that money could be spent? Think about food, lodging, staff time...
- The negotiators can vote to stay late or go home - but unless that's done by secret ballot, there will be social pressure to conform to the group's decision. Is that actually consensual?
- We only pay for childcare during business hours. When negotiators who have caretaking duties need to leave, who is representing their members' unique perspectives and needs?
- The expectation of Push Week requires that our negotiators have a certain amount of privilege and flexibility. If you have a second job, care-taking duties, or a disability that precludes participating in Push Week, you're probably not going to sign up to be a negotiator - even if you'd be great at it.

**In summary, participating in Push Week creates an environment where our negotiators are subject to inhumane working conditions, we lose a lot of time and money while MMB faffs about, and we get a contract that is more likely to have mistakes and less likely to include solutions that reflect the diverse needs and perspectives of our members.**

**So... Why are we doing this again?**
